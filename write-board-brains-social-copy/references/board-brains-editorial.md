# Board Brains editorial reference

## Brand perspective

Board Brains is Madeline and Ollie's board game project. Treat their combined perspective as the differentiator:

- Madeline often considers play through connection, self-understanding, accessibility, neuro-affirming resourcing, and how an experience feels for a particular brain.
- Ollie often considers systems, mechanics, product and graphic design, constraints, mastery, and how the pieces create a satisfying experience.
- Together, they treat games as designed experiences and as a bridge to connection, restoration, and meaningful play.
- Their work goes beyond conventional reviews by helping people understand how a game actually feels to play and whether it may fit them.

Use this perspective as an editorial lens, not mandatory copy. Do not force the mission, neurodivergence, or both creators into a post when the transcript does not support it.

Official context: https://www.theboardbrains.com/about

## Voice

Write in a casual, friendly, direct voice with a cheeky sense of humour.

- Prefer familiar words, contractions, short paragraphs, and spoken rhythms.
- Let humour emerge from the specific situation, honest opinion, or self-awareness.
- Preserve nuance and uncertainty when the speaker is genuinely undecided.
- Prefer concrete observations over polished marketing claims.
- Use simple punctuation. Never use em dashes.
- Keep supporting copy succinct because viewers will usually scan it.

Do not imitate generic creator language. Avoid patterns such as:

- "You won't believe what happened next."
- "This changes everything."
- "Here's why you need this game."
- "Calling all board game lovers."
- Manufactured outrage, false jeopardy, or an unsupported superlative.
- A generic recap that removes the transcript's tension or personality.
- A forced joke added only because the voice is described as cheeky.

Self-aware exaggeration is welcome when the context makes the joke legible. Attention-grabbing copy may borrow the shape of clickbait, but its promise must be sincere and paid off in the video.

## Editorial precedent: solo adventure

### Transcript signals

Ollie is alone for ten days while Madeline is away. He wants viewers to help prioritise several games for solo play. Across the transcript, he repeatedly questions whether each game's appeal comes from its mechanics or from the shared laughter, competition, and interaction around the table.

### Chosen primary hook

> Madeline left me alone for 10 days and I’m worried I might not like board games without her.

### Chosen caption

> Madeline left me alone for 10 days and I’m worried I might not like board games without her.
>
> I’ve got a list of games I want to try solo this week and I genuinely don’t know how some of them are going to hold up. For a few of them I’m pretty optimistic. For others I’m not sure how much of what I love about them is actually the mechanics and how much of it is just the chaos and laughter of everyone suffering together.
>
> Which one would you put at the top of the list?

### Durable lessons

- Choose the deeper personal tension over the most obvious list-based summary when the transcript supports it.
- Use a specific real circumstance to make an abstract question immediate.
- Let self-aware exaggeration carry the humour without creating a false premise.
- Expand the hook's tension in the caption without naming or listing the games viewers are meant to discover in the video.
- Retain uncertainty so the video contains the answer or exploration.
- Always end the caption with a specific prompt that continues the video's conversation rather than attaching generic engagement bait.
